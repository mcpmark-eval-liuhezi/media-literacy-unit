### Notes on the count

The line "Never gonna give you up" appears **6** times in the transcript, once at the head of each of the six full chorus blocks. The bridge fragments — "Never gonna give, never gonna give / (Give you up)" — are a broken-up variant of the line and are deliberately **not** counted, since they are not the exact phrase. This distinction makes a good classroom discussion point about what counts as a "hook" in lyric analysis.

## Takeaway

"Never Gonna Give You Up" became the internet's favorite bait-and-switch because the rickroll works on a mismatch of registers: a sincere, glossy 1987 pop song delivered as a punchline, so the victim's disappointment and the song's earnestness play off each other every single time. The hook's relentless repetition — the phrase returns in every chorus — makes the gag instantly recognizable from its very first syllables, so even three notes are enough to deliver the joke. It is a near-perfect teaching example of how viral media spread through low-cost participation, shared in-jokes, and the pleasure of being in on the trick rather than falling for it.
